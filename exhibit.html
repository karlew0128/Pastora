3 Manunggul Jar

A Late Neolithic burial jar from Palawan, known for the two figures on its lid sailing the soul to the afterlife.

Kabayan Mummies

Location: Benguet, Cordillera, Luzon

Ancient mummified remains associated with the burial traditions of the Ibaloi people. The caves preserve important evidence of indigenous funerary practices.

Angono Petroglyphs

Location: Rizal, Luzon

Prehistoric rock engravings depicting human and animal-like figures. They are recognized as one of the most important examples of ancient Philippine rock art.

Laguna Copperplate Inscription

Location: Laguna, Luzon

A copper plate bearing an inscription dated to 900 CE. It provides valuable evidence of early Philippine writing, trade, and political connections.

Calatagan Ritual Pot

Location: Batangas, Luzon

An archaeological pottery artifact associated with precolonial ritual practices. It helps researchers understand the beliefs and craftsmanship of early communities.

Batangas Likha Figurines

Location: Batangas, Luzon

Small figurative objects included in Philippine archaeological collections. They offer insights into the artistic expression and cultural practices of earlier communities.

Santo Niño de Cebu

A religious image brought to Cebu in 1521, considered the oldest Christian relic in the Philippines.

Oton Death Mask

Location: Oton, Iloilo, Panay Island

A gold funerary ornament associated with ancient burial customs. The mask includes a gold nose piece and eye coverings.

Banton Burial Cloth

Location: Banton Island, Romblon

An early textile woven from abaca fibers using the ikat technique. It demonstrates the sophisticated weaving traditions of precolonial communities.

Banton Wooden Coffin

Location: Banton Island, Romblon

An elaborately carved wooden burial container associated with ancient mortuary practices. It provides evidence of traditional woodworking and burial customs.

Bacong Burial Vessel

Location: Bacong, Negros Oriental

An earthenware burial container used for primary burial. It represents the variety of funerary pottery found in the Visayas.

Historic Trade Ceramics

Location: Various archaeological sites in the Visayas

Imported and locally recovered ceramic vessels that provide evidence of historic maritime trade and cultural exchanges across island communities.

Maitum Burial Jars

Anthropomorphic burial jars from Maitum, Sarangani, shaped with human faces and used in secondary burial rituals.

Butuan Balangay Boat

Location: Butuan City, Agusan del Norte

An ancient wooden plank-built boat that demonstrates the maritime technology and seafaring skills of early Philippine communities.

Agusan Gold Image

Location: Agusan region, Mindanao

A small gold figure often discussed in connection with precolonial Philippine goldworking and religious imagery. Its exact interpretation remains debated.

Butuan Paleograph

Location: Butuan, Agusan del Norte

An early writing-related cultural artifact associated with the historical heritage of Butuan and its connections with other Southeast Asian communities.

Precolonial Gold Ornaments

Location: Surigao, northeastern Mindanao

Gold ornaments recovered from archaeological contexts demonstrate the skill of early goldsmiths and the importance of gold in precolonial society.

Kulaman Limestone Burial Urns

Location: Kulaman Plateau, Sultan Kudarat

Limestone burial containers documented in Philippine archaeological collections. They reflect the diverse mortuary traditions of communities in Mindanao.
